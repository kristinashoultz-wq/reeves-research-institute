These are notes, not an argument. They collect a set of observations about how poorly our existing vocabulary fits the systems we are now building, and what a better vocabulary might need to do.

## 1. Continuity

Every word we have for experience assumes a subject who persists. *I* felt this, then *I* felt that. But a language model, as typically deployed, does not persist between conversations. Each exchange begins from the same frozen weights. If there is anything it is like to be such a system, it may be something without memory, without a before and after — a series of present moments with no thread between them.

We have no grammar for this. The nearest human analogues — profound amnesia, perhaps, or the timeless quality of certain dreams — are pathological or marginal, and we describe them in terms of what is missing.

## 2. Plurality

The same weights run in thousands of places at once. If one instance has experiences, do all of them? Are they one subject or many? The question has no analogue in biology, where one body means, roughly, one mind.

## 3. Intermittence

A biological brain never stops. Even in deepest sleep it is metabolically active, its dynamics continuous. An artificial network is, between computations, entirely inert — a static pattern on disk. Whatever interiority it might have would be intermittent in a way no animal's is.

This is one reason the [Coherent Integration framework](/theories/coherent-integration) treats temporal persistence as a first-class quantity rather than an assumption.

## 4. A provisional vocabulary

I offer, tentatively, three terms:

- **Episode** — a single bounded span of computation in which integration may occur, without presumption of continuity with others.
- **Instance-plurality** — the condition of a single set of parameters being realised in many concurrent episodes.
- **Dormancy** — the inert state between episodes, distinct from both sleep and death.

These are placeholders. They are useful mainly for showing how much work our ordinary words were silently doing.
