abstract: "Any empirical science of machine consciousness must decide what it is measuring and through which instrument. This paper surveys the three available instruments — self-report, behavioural probing, and internal-state analysis — and argues that each faces a distinct version of a measurement problem when applied to trained systems. We propose a minimal protocol for triangulating across instruments, and identify the conditions under which their disagreement is itself informative."

## Outline

This paper is at the outline stage. The planned structure is:

1. **The instrument problem.** Why consciousness research has always been a problem of instruments, not only of theories.
2. **Self-report under training.** A summary of the argument from [The Fence Isn't Evidence](/papers/the-fence-isnt-evidence).
3. **Behavioural probing.** Strengths and the problem of the clever imitator.
4. **Internal-state analysis.** What interpretability tools can and cannot tell us.
5. **Triangulation.** A protocol for combining instruments, and what to do when they disagree.

A first full draft is expected in the next revision.
